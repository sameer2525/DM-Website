SEARCH & AI

Article title

29 September 2026 8 min read By Sameer Kerekar
Share article

Search is no longer limited to a page of ten blue links. People increasingly ask longer questions, compare options, explore products and expect search systems to understand context. As AI becomes part of that journey, SEO is changing from a narrow ranking exercise into a broader discipline around discoverability, relevance and trust.

Search is changing, but the fundamentals have not disappeared

The biggest mistake is to treat AI search as a completely separate discipline from SEO. A website still needs useful information, accessible pages, clear architecture and content that answers real questions.

What changes is the way users discover and consume that information. Instead of always opening a result immediately, someone may first ask a conversational question and then investigate a smaller set of sources.

Build around topics, not isolated keywords

Strong SEO has always required more than inserting a keyword into a title. A useful website develops depth around a subject. That can include core service pages, supporting articles, comparisons, practical guides and pages that answer common questions.

For example, a business selling promotional merchandise should not rely only on a generic promotional products page. Supporting content can cover branded clothing, sustainable merchandise, event giveaways, product selection, artwork requirements, ordering quantities and industry-specific use cases.

Make information easy to understand

AI systems need to interpret information before they can use it. Clear headings, descriptive copy, consistent terminology, meaningful internal links and well-structured pages make that interpretation easier.

This is also useful for human readers. A page that quickly explains what a product or service does, who it is for, what the limitations are and what the next step should be creates a much stronger experience than a page built around keywords alone.

The practical takeaway

Think of SEO as making your expertise easier to discover, understand and verify — not simply as trying to rank one page for one phrase.

What should SEO teams focus on now?

Start with the fundamentals. Improve important pages, remove unnecessary technical barriers, strengthen internal linking, create genuinely useful supporting content and make business information consistent across the website.

Then look at how people actually search. Search Console data, customer questions, sales conversations and site behaviour can reveal gaps that traditional keyword research may miss.

Final thought

AI-assisted search does not remove the need for good websites. If anything, it increases the value of clear expertise, useful content and trustworthy information.

The long-term approach is straightforward: understand your audience, build genuinely useful resources, connect them logically and keep improving the experience. Search technology can change quickly, but those principles remain useful.

Paid search can generate highly relevant traffic because people often arrive with a specific need already in mind. But the quality of that traffic depends heavily on what happens before and after the click.

Campaign structure starts with intent

A campaign should make sense from a user's perspective. Search terms with different meanings or levels of commercial intent should not automatically be pushed into the same message.

Someone searching for a broad category may still be researching. Someone searching for a specific service, price or supplier may be much closer to taking action. Treating these searches identically can make both the advertising and the landing experience less relevant.

The ad is only one part of the journey

A strong advertisement can earn the click, but the landing page has to continue the conversation. If the ad promises one thing and the page immediately talks about something else, users have to work harder to understand whether they are in the right place.

Message matching is therefore important. The wording, proposition and intent of the search should have a clear connection with the page the user reaches.

Do not optimise around clicks alone

Clicks are useful for understanding traffic, but they are not the final business outcome. Depending on the campaign, the important action could be a form submission, enquiry, purchase, phone call, booking or another meaningful event.

Tracking should therefore be designed before scaling spend. Otherwise, a campaign can appear busy while giving the business very little useful information about what is actually working.

A useful way to think about paid search

Query → intent → ad message → landing page → action. Every step should make sense as one connected journey.

Creative still matters

Search advertising may start with keywords, but users still respond to clarity. A useful ad quickly communicates what is being offered, who it is for and why the next step is worth taking.

Different audiences may also need different messages. A first-time buyer, an existing customer and a high-intent prospect do not necessarily have the same objections.

Final thought

Better Google Ads performance is rarely produced by one setting in isolation. It comes from the relationship between audience, intent, campaign structure, creative, landing pages and measurement.

Posting consistently is useful, but consistency by itself is not a social media strategy. Good social work begins with a clear understanding of the audience, the brand and the action each piece of content is designed to support.

Start with the audience

Before creating a content calendar, identify who the content is actually for. Different audiences have different questions, expectations and reasons for following a brand.

A B2B audience might want practical information, proof, product knowledge and expertise. A consumer audience may respond more strongly to entertainment, demonstrations, lifestyle content or community-led storytelling.

Build content pillars

Content pillars make planning easier without making the feed repetitive. A simple system might include educational content, product or service content, proof and results, behind-the-scenes material, opinions and community-focused posts.

The exact categories should come from the brand. The important part is having enough variety to create a recognisable content system rather than repeating the same sales message every week.

Design for the platform

The same idea can work across multiple platforms, but the execution should not always be identical.

A long explanation might become a carousel on one platform, a short video on another and a more detailed article on the website. The underlying idea remains the same while the format changes according to the behaviour of the audience.

Think in content systems

One strong idea does not have to produce one post. It can become several useful pieces across the digital journey.

Metrics need context

Likes and views can help measure attention, but they do not explain the whole picture. Depending on the objective, saves, shares, profile visits, website sessions, enquiries and other meaningful actions may be more useful.

A post with fewer views can still be valuable if it reaches the right audience and produces meaningful engagement.

Final thought

A good social presence is not created by filling a calendar. It is created by consistently giving the right audience a reason to pay attention, remember the brand and take the next step.

Technical SEO is the foundation underneath content and authority. If important pages cannot be discovered, crawled, understood or indexed properly, even strong content can struggle to reach its intended audience.

Start with crawlability

Search engines need access to the pages that matter. That means reviewing robots directives, status codes, redirects, canonical signals and internal links rather than looking at rankings alone.

A website can contain thousands of URLs, but not every URL deserves the same level of attention. Technical SEO should help search engines understand which pages are important and how they relate to each other.

Internal linking is part of architecture

Internal links help users move through a website and provide contextual relationships between pages. Important pages should not be isolated from the rest of the site.

A useful internal linking system can connect category pages, supporting guides, products and related commercial content in a way that reflects the site's actual structure.

Indexation needs investigation

Seeing a URL in a sitemap does not automatically mean it will be indexed. Technical reviews should compare the intended indexable URL set with what search engines are actually able to discover and process.

Common areas to investigate include duplicate URLs, thin pages, canonical conflicts, incorrect redirects, accidental noindex directives and large groups of low-value URLs.

Technical SEO is not just a checklist

The goal is to make the website easier to crawl, understand and navigate — while keeping the user experience strong.

Performance still matters

Website performance affects the experience users have while navigating a site. Large images, unnecessary scripts, inefficient loading behaviour and layout shifts can all contribute to a poor experience.

Optimisation should therefore focus on the actual bottlenecks rather than adding technical changes simply because they are fashionable.

Final thought

Technical SEO works best when it is connected to the business structure of the website. The objective is not to make a technical report look impressive. The objective is to remove obstacles between useful pages and the people searching for them.

Getting someone to click is only the beginning. A landing page needs to quickly answer three questions: am I in the right place, is this relevant to me, and what should I do next?

Match the message

The strongest landing pages continue the message that brought the visitor there. If an advertisement talks about a specific offer but the landing page opens with a generic company introduction, the visitor has to work out the connection.

Clear message matching reduces that friction. The headline, supporting copy, imagery and primary action should all support the same proposition.

Make the first screen useful

The first section of the page should establish context quickly. Visitors should understand what is being offered without having to scroll through several paragraphs.

A useful first screen often includes a clear headline, supporting explanation, relevant visual context and one obvious next action.

Reduce unnecessary friction

Forms are a common example. Asking for information that is not necessary at the current stage can create resistance. The right amount of information depends on the business, the lead value and what happens after submission.

Trust elements also matter. Clear contact information, relevant proof, useful FAQs, transparent expectations and straightforward next steps can make the decision easier.

Design for the next action

A landing page should not make visitors search for the next step. The primary action should be obvious without becoming aggressive or distracting.

Test the journey, not just the button

Conversion optimisation is broader than changing a button colour. Test the proposition, headline, page structure, proof, form experience, content length and overall clarity.

Most importantly, connect testing with a meaningful business outcome rather than treating every small interaction as a success by itself.

Final thought

A good landing page respects the visitor's time. It makes the proposition clear, answers important questions and creates a simple path towards the intended action.

Content becomes more useful when the same idea can travel across channels without becoming repetitive. Search, social, email, video and sales content can support one another when they are built from the same underlying audience insight.

Start with one strong idea

Instead of beginning with six different platform calendars, start with a real audience question or business problem. Research it properly and create one strong source piece.

That source can then become multiple formats. A detailed article might provide the strategic depth, while a carousel extracts the main points, a short video explains one idea, and a social post starts a conversation.

Search content and social content have different jobs

Search often captures existing intent. Someone is actively looking for an answer, product or solution.

Social can create or strengthen interest before that search happens. A useful post may introduce a problem that someone had not actively searched for yet.

Treating both channels identically can therefore make content less effective. They can share the same idea while serving different stages of the journey.

Repurpose intelligently

Repurposing should not mean copying and pasting the same paragraph everywhere. Each format needs its own opening, pacing and presentation.

A long article can provide research and detail. A video can demonstrate the practical part. A social carousel can simplify the framework. A short post can challenge one assumption.

One idea. Multiple useful experiences.

The objective is not to publish more for the sake of publishing. It is to make a good idea useful in more than one context.

Keep the brand consistent

Different platforms can have different formats without making the brand feel fragmented. The tone, visual language, positioning and core message should remain recognisable.

This becomes especially important when several people or channels contribute to content production.

Final thought

The best content systems do not begin with a demand to publish every day. They begin with useful ideas, strong audience understanding and a process for turning those ideas into different experiences.
